## Design decisions (behavioural)

- **D1 — One mapping, transcribed from the tables.** Each of the 21
  colour tokens and 6 typography tokens maps to named Office
  properties; the mapping is a test-table, not scattered code — a
  missing row is a failing test.
- **D2 — Hatch patterns as patterns, not simulated.** Procurement
  hatch maps to the Office pattern facility (`Fill.Pattern` — exact
  member probed at Tier-A upgrade) with `HatchPitchPt`/`HatchLinePt`
  honoured as far as the facility allows; any deviation is recorded
  and equivalence-tested in R8.3 rather than silently accepted.
- **D3 — Colour verbatim.** `#RRGGBB`/`#RRGGBBAA` → Office RGB/alpha
  with no gamma or theme interference (theme colours are never used);
  transitivity with the PNG renderer is R8.3's job, but the values
  here are the pinned source of truth.
- **D4 — Live comparison is against the tables.** The gate compares
  every rendered style family (planned/actual/baseline/critical/
  delay/procurement×3/custom/milestone×4/delineator/splitter/bands/
  grid) to the entity-guide initial values — the "approved visual
  specification" (the tables carry the revision-3 status: initial
  defaults; product-owner review updates them together with tests).

## Acceptance tests

## Stop conditions (item-specific)

- A token cannot be expressed on the host (pattern pitch granularity,
  alpha support) → stop; record the gap; R8.3's equivalence policy
  needs the fact, never a silent approximation.
- The live comparison diverges from the tables → stop; either the
  mapping or the render is wrong — no "close enough".

## Risk and rollback

Main risk: matrix drift when the tables change — mitigated by the
transcription rule (rows re-read at Step 0) and the unmapped-token
guard. Rollback: `git revert`.
